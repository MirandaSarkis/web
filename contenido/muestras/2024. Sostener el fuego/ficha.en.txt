Subtítulo: Eight passages on the work of Miranda Sarkis
Firma: Verónica Gómez, September 2024. Translated from the Spanish
---
1. The veins of clay are sought right by National Route 75, in Sanagasta, in the province of La Rioja. From the city of Córdoba, where she now lives, Miranda Sarkis travels some 500 kilometers to meet her material. The car is borrowed. She brings a small bottle of water, boxes, a rake, a shovel, heavy-duty bags, the kind used for rubble. She brings company. Tasks and weights have to be shared out. Once, her companions were her parents, the same ones who brought her into the world on those red lands, in 1994.

2. From those red lands, Miranda brings into the world forms that become ceramics in the fire and that will later be suspended in the air, in this dark room, in another province, with earth of another color: Rafaela, Santa Fe.

3. To get the clay and know whether it will work, you have to pour a little stream of water and knead, do the elasticity test. If it breaks, it is discarded; if it withstands the stretching, it is stockpiled. The clay goes into boxes. They are weighed. They are divided into 10-kilo lots. They are sent to Córdoba by mail. The young man at the post office is struck by the weight of the boxes.

4. What drawings do our works trace before they reach their destination, what is the map of their wanderings, the comings and goings, the bonds of affection that are like ropes pulled taut there where the wind falls silent and the ears go on ringing.

5. Stones, roots, thorns, branches, plastic waste, small plants. As the earth passes through the sieve, they are left out of the equation. And yet they insist on returning to Miranda's hands in the form of a stone given by a friend, a gift of black mica, a few pieces of root or branch that eluded the sieve's discrimination. These inlays cause cracks or drawings in the firing, something like the murmur of memory on the surfaces, a rough and honest treasure that the fingers run over and long to identify, an anchor point in the ancestral vastness.

6. The pieces are fired with wood and charcoal in a demountable kiln of 200 bricks that Miranda built over her grill. The bricks, of irregular sizes and rugged faces, leave gaps through which one can spy on the fire. During the wait, the artist is hypnotized by those glowing peepholes, underlined by the smoke-darkened bricks. While the kiln gives its secret its final form, she remains almost blind, as we are now, in this darkened room, sounding out hollows that urge us to pass through the crusts and rediscover the constructive quality of the gaze. Like the river to its bed, the eye clings to imaginative observation in an act of poetic survival.

7. These pieces let us look through. Even, in some cases, magnify the detail with a lens. That is how images multiply as if by magic. Telluric spyglasses, bottomless vessels, exoskeletons, clay splints, nests. Every association and none. The grace of Miranda Sarkis's pieces lies in a fitting combination of the associative latency of form and the precision of its function.

8. Potter wasps, termites and horneros (ovenbirds) build their nests with saliva, earth, plant fibers, small stones. Oscillating between tubular and spherical forms, more or less open, more or less irregular, geometric perfection gives way to the need for shelter and to a craft of small, patient additions whose scale is set by the size of the beak and the number of builders. Miranda Sarkis's sculptures preserve the scale of the hand, the close relationship with the body, nourishing that political-affective stance of artists who choose to bring huggable objects into the world.
