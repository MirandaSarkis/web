Firma: Miranda Sarkis
---
I woke up and I knew: this was the day. I grabbed a pickaxe and a shovel and started the car. Dawn was breaking; the first rays of sun could be seen between the mountains.

I reached the foot of the greenest hill; the sun now lit almost everything. I got out of the car with the tools over my shoulder and began to walk. A chorus of coyoyo cicadas and birds scored the scene. When I found the place that felt right, the blow of the pickaxe against the hard earth began to accompany them. At one point I could hear nothing but my own heavy breathing.

By the afternoon I considered the work almost done. I climbed into the pit and lay down. I was surprised by how straight the walls were. I had managed to dig an almost perfect rectangle, roomy; the barely damp earth caressed my back. Roots and stones, held in the walls, surround me.

A tear left my right eye and ended its journey in the center of my ear. It never touched the earth.

“It can't be,” I said, and I began to cry.

I had never cried so much, and yet the earth absorbed all my tears. My eyes hurt; my nose bothered me, blocked by a kind of improvised mud. I could barely make out the luminous sky, which was beginning to fill with stars.

My only comfort was that the nearest river might rise and cover me. That the fresh water might mix with the salt.

It didn't happen.

I fell into a deep sleep, held by the four walls of earth and the sky up high. I woke with my body aching. A new day.
