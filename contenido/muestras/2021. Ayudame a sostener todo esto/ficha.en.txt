Epígrafe: “I always want the assurance of at least thinking that I understand; I do not know how to surrender to disorientation.” Clarice Lispector, The Passion According to G.H.
Firma: Hugo Albrieu, translated from the Spanish
---
Perhaps it was the mandatory isolation of 2020, in the midst of a global pandemic, that led Miranda to produce all the works in this exhibition. Her choice to spend it in the hills of Córdoba, in Agua de Oro, in the middle of nowhere, surrounded by stones and a river that all but dies in winter, which makes the landscape quite similar to her native La Rioja. A veiled way of returning home, the protective need for origins, the stability of drawing. Drawing as an originary core. It would seem that only art is capable of situating the present time and offering it shelter.

She looks at a Thonet chair and draws it in different situations, forcing a balance. She looks at it with that revolution Pizarnik speaks of, until her eyes are pulverized. She uses ink, watercolor and graphite, and chooses paper as her support. She brings in the stones around her. She needs a ballast, something with an even greater weight to hold her to the ground, to let her harmonize the spaces, to find the exact point where inner and outer coexistence get along. More or less. Pushing resistance to its limit. A rebellious and conservative act.

With expressive urgency she takes photographs, films, and installs the objects chair and stone in different situations. The lack of a space in which to work is not an obstacle; it is a challenge. Without a doubt, paper, and the outdoors as an experimental field, gradually bring her order. She limits her colors to red and black; she needs no more to cover anything. The drawing holds itself up, through its purity, its nobility. That refined, clean image is the result of a process of searching for balance within chaos, as a philosophy of life, as a performative practice. A need to tame the entropy of her everyday life.

There are places that last only an instant; they are those singular moments that only art can capture. Can disorder be sensed within order? Can a certain peace be suggested from within unrest? Art is the means of making the simultaneity of contradiction possible.
