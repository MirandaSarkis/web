A Summer at Home is a series of drawings that grows out of a visual story made up of small stories. I don't know which came first, the writing or the image; I do know that the words appeared on the paper almost without thinking. Each phrase found its place among clouds and seas, stones and mountains, branches and words.

One drawing asks the other a question; the other answers. They are all in dialogue, some of them implicitly.

Paper and graphite were the most fitting materials for the series. As in a letter, I began by writing, imitating typewriter type. A mechanical, repetitive movement. A futile attempt to make each letter identical to its pair. By drawing, I explore the landscape through ritual and writing.

A Summer at Home is about going back to the house of one's father and mother, and asking oneself questions. Sitting down to contemplate the earth of the patio, trying to find what connects us. Walking between the walls that watched us grow up and trying to remember what it was like.

I thought that going back in summer would clear up some questions. Now I not only have more questions, but also more drawings and writings, in which I try to find the answers.
