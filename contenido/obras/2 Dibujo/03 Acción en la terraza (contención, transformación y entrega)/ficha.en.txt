---
This triptych starts from a simple gesture: the encounter between a hand and a stone. Across the three images a sequence unfolds that is not linear but cyclical and symbolic: how a mineral is extracted, how it is held, how it is cared for and how, eventually, it breaks.

The first image presents the most direct action: one hand holds a tool, the other a nail or awl. There is an intention to pierce, to open, to enter. Here the tension between force and resistance is set in motion, between the body that insists and the matter that defends itself.

In the second, the hands curve in a gesture of containment. The void between them suggests something invisible yet present: an imaginary stone, the echo of what is being protected. It is an image that speaks of preservation, of care, even of waiting.

The third image closes the cycle with an offering. The open hands hold the same void, but now with a different disposition: it is no longer about protecting, but about giving. That blank space, which runs through all three scenes, becomes the protagonist. It is what cannot be seen and yet structures the whole action.

Through simple, repeated lines, the drawing insists on the idea that the mineral, too, has a life: it resists, responds, transforms. This work is a reflection on how we relate to the materials we extract from the earth, and on how that relationship always involves an unstable balance between violence and reverence.
