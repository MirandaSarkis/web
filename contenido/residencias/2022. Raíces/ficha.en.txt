I arrived in Aconquija, Catamarca, for my first residency with many projects written down in a logbook and some materials just in case: watercolors, papers of different kinds and sizes, pencils, inks, a camera. I ended up using none of it. Before producing anything, I wanted to connect with the place, to get to know it.

During the first days we shared activities with the other residents and the residency's guides, and we met people from the village who shared their knowledge and trades with us. One day we met Carda, who taught us about the native plants of the area; with her guidance we gathered plants and made sahumos, bundles of herbs for burning.

After that exchange I began going out to walk alone through the village. In truth I was never alone: I was almost always accompanied by one of the local dogs, and others joined along the way. On those walks I began to gather native plants, some of them in bloom, always asking permission before cutting them and giving thanks for their abundance.

With them I began to build a giant sahumo. I decided it had to be circular: like life, like repetition, like the cycles of nature, like the way we humans work. We are cyclical beings.

Aconquija is a striking territory, ranging from the yungas cloud forest to desert, with rivers, mountains and snow. To install the sahumo I chose the most desert-like part, perhaps because it reminded me of my own lands, reddish and brown: imposing walls of dry mud. We carried the sahumo there and photographed it installed in that landscape.

Walking a little farther, we came upon a kind of wasteland between the mud walls, by the side of the road. It was hard and sad to see how we humans throw things away without caring about the consequences of what we do. We decided that this would be the place for the burning: if any place needed a cleansing, it was that one.

We hung the sahumo from the bridge that carries the road, lit a few candles and began to set it alight. The fire spread through the dry plants that ran around the circle, and the smoke began to rise and take its place in the air.

Thank you Joel, Gera, Ana and Flor for accompanying me in this experience.
