My practice is born of a displacement: the one I make, again and again, between the place where I was born, La Rioja, and the place where I live and work, Córdoba. That coming and going is not just a biographical fact; it is the way I produce and think about my work.

I travel alone to the mountains of La Rioja in search of clay. I gather it, carry it away, and process it far from where it came from. To me, that earth is not a neutral material: it is a fragment of displaced landscape, charged with memory and time. Working with it means sustaining a bond with the territory I come from, even at a distance, and putting the idea of rootedness under tension.

Words also emerge from those same journeys. I write a story, at once fictional and autobiographical, which I then transfer to clay: I model stones and engrave a word on each one, until together they reconstruct the text. Writing and matter are, for me, two forms of the same gesture: carrying something from one place to another without it losing its origin.

Drawing and installation are central axes of my work. Through them I bring into play, and into question, everything that runs through my work: the way I occupy space, the way I arrange matter and lead the viewer's body to come closer, to look and to move. Installation lets me think of the work as a territory in itself; drawing lets me trace by hand what words and earth cannot quite say.

The resulting pieces are fragile, cracked, porous. They function as viewfinders, hollow bodies, spaces of passage: places that light passes through, and that reveal both the persistence of an origin and the impossibility of ever fully belonging.
